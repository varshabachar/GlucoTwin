The alarm said she was fine. 24%, green, stable.

Then GlucoTwin looked at what she was about to eat. Rice, dal and sabzi — rice first,
projected peak 146, crosses the line. Dal and sabzi first, 135, stays under. Same plate,
same afternoon, on a patient nothing had flagged.

It's a digital twin per patient: a FHIR health record fused with CGM, wearable and meal
data, learning that person meal by meal — predict the peak before they eat, compare with
what the sensor recorded, re-fit. Across 431 real meals that cut the error by 22%.

And it knows what it doesn't know. Insulin sensitivity falls in the luteal phase, so the
same advice stops working — but every woman in this cohort is post-menopausal, so that
module ships labelled a capability demo, not a finding.

Happiest Health Digital Twin Challenge 2026. Open data, leave-one-subject-out, and a
script that proves the twin never sees the future.

A glucose monitor tells you that you crossed 140. It tells you at the exact moment it
happens — which is the one moment you can no longer do anything about it.

GlucoTwin warns two hours earlier, and then tells you what would change it.

It builds a digital twin of each patient from their health record plus live glucose,
wearable and meal data, and learns that person meal by meal: predict the peak before
they eat, compare with what the sensor recorded, refit.

AUROC 0.612 → 0.728. False alarms cut from nearly seven a day to two. 86% of spikes
caught, a median 45 minutes ahead. And across 431 real meals, the error in the predicted
peak fell 22%.

Then the part a risk score can't do: rice first, projected 146 — dal and sabzi first,
135. Same plate, same afternoon, on a patient the alarm had already called stable.

Built for the Happiest Health Digital Twin Challenge 2026. Open data,
leave-one-subject-out, and a script that proves the twin never sees the future.

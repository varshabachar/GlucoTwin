A glucose monitor tells you that you crossed 140. It tells you at the exact moment it
happens — which is the one moment you can no longer do anything about it.

136 million people in India have prediabetes. Most of them are somebody's parents. And
most of them get told the same thing: cut the rice, cut the sugar, cut everything.
Advice that is easy to give and hard to live with.

GlucoTwin warns two hours earlier, and then tells you what would actually change it. It
builds a digital twin of each patient from their health record plus live glucose,
wearable and meal data, and learns that person meal by meal: predict the peak before they
eat, compare with what the sensor recorded, refit.

AUROC 0.612 → 0.728. False alarms cut from nearly seven a day to two. 86% of spikes
caught, a median 45 minutes ahead. Across 431 real meals, the error in the predicted peak
fell 22%.

So it doesn't ask her to stop eating rice. It asks her to eat the dal first — and for one
patient the alarm had already called stable, that is 146 against 135. Same dinner, same
table, nothing taken away.

Built for the Happiest Health Digital Twin Challenge 2026. Open data,
leave-one-subject-out, and a script that proves the twin never sees the future.
